What counts as hacking

Internet hacking is the unauthorized access, manipulation, or exploitation of digital devices, computer systems, and network connections. It happens when someone finds and uses a weakness in software, hardware, or human behavior to steal data, disrupt services, or take control of a system.

#6 Optimizely (website experimentation platform)

Nov 16, 2026
CriticalTechRetailFinance

Attackers exploited a flaw in Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform to inject malicious JavaScript into scripts that run on customers' websites. Researchers at Report URI found the code on 178 organizations' sites, including big names like Salesforce, Zoom, HSBC, CNN and Ikea, so visitors to those sites were exposed without the companies themselves being breached.

Impact

Per Report URI, the malware swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed payment cards, stole cookies and screenshots, and tried to deliver iOS exploit kits to iPhones and iPads; at least $7,000 in crypto was traced as stolen. Most of the malicious code was removed the same day.

Status

Confirmed by Optimizely. In a Nov. 18 update it said under 5% of its customers were affected, the flaw (in its Web Experimentation sign-up flow) was closed within three hours of detection, and all injected code has been removed.

#1 FAA: nationwide ground stop

Nov 13, 2026
CriticalUnconfirmed / speculationAviationGovernment

The FAA halted departures across the U.S. for more than four hours after its air traffic communications network became unstable. It came one day after the big Microsoft outage, so many people asked whether the two were linked or whether someone had hacked the system.

What people speculated

A hack or coordinated attack on air traffic control, possibly connected to the Microsoft outage the day before.

Official explanation

The FAA says it has "no indication that this was a cyberattack or an outside nefarious actor." Its explanation: after a contractor installed a new router at a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, data flooded the FAA's legacy network (with a second problem site in Amarillo, Texas). Officials said it was not related to the Microsoft outage.

Impact

Instability at 17 of 22 en route air traffic control centers; about 12,462 flights canceled that day (FAA figure).

Status

No evidence of a hack. Root-cause investigation ongoing.

About and methodology

How confirmed and speculated are decided

  • Confirmed Hacks were confirmed by the affected organization, a government agency, or law enforcement. Numbers come from those statements, filings, or the linked reporting. Attacker claims are labeled as claims.
  • Speculated / Potential Hacks are outages or incidents where the public wondered about a hack. They are not confirmed hacks. Each one shows what the organization or investigators officially said.

How this tracker is updated

  • The list is reviewed and updated about every two hours during the day, Eastern Time.
  • Sorted by scale and impact, biggest first. Severity is a rough editorial rating, not an official score.
  • Covers roughly the past 12 months, plus a few older events that are still widely referenced. Always check the linked sources for the latest.

The definition of hacking used on this site is at the top of the page, under What counts as hacking.
