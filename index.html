What counts as hacking

Internet hacking is the unauthorized access, manipulation, or exploitation of digital devices, computer systems, and network connections. It happens when someone finds and uses a weakness in software, hardware, or human behavior to steal data, disrupt services, or take control of a system.

#2 Credit Mutuel Arkea (bank, France)

Nov 4, 2026
CriticalFinance

Attackers got in through the platform of one of the bank's service providers and used Crédit Mutuel Arkéa's own account to send money to other banks, which was then converted into cryptocurrency. The bank suspended some transactions as a precaution and its app was unavailable for several hours.

Confirmed by Crédit Mutuel Arkéa. Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation, led by France's anti-cybercrime office (OFAC). Recovery efforts ongoing.

#8 Optimizely (website experimentation platform)

Nov 16, 2026
CriticalTechRetailFinance

Attackers exploited a flaw in Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform to inject malicious JavaScript into scripts that run on customers' websites. Researchers at Report URI found the code on 178 organizations' sites, including big names like Salesforce, Zoom, HSBC, CNN and Ikea, so visitors to those sites were exposed without the companies themselves being breached.

Impact

Per Report URI, the malware swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed payment cards, stole cookies and screenshots, and tried to deliver iOS exploit kits to iPhones and iPads; at least $7,000 in crypto was traced as stolen. Most of the malicious code was removed the same day.

Status

Confirmed by Optimizely. In a Nov. 18 update it said under 5% of its customers were affected, the flaw (in its Web Experimentation sign-up flow) was closed within three hours of detection, and all injected code has been removed.

#9 PLAID KARTE customers (MUFG Bank, JINS, Shimamura and others, Japan)

Nov 13, 2026
CriticalTechFinanceRetail

Attackers exploited a vulnerability in servers run by PLAID, the Tokyo company behind the KARTE customer-data marketing service, stole credentials used to connect to its systems and databases, and read data out. They also took over a PLAID account to get into the admin console. PLAID shut down all KARTE services on Nov. 13, and companies that use KARTE have been announcing leaks of their own customers' data since.

Impact

Varies by company. MUFG Bank: about 6.5 million records of gender, age and region (no names or contact details). JINS: up to about 18.29 million accounts (email, surname, birth date and more). Shimamura: about 7 million members' birth dates, gender, prefecture and postal code. RIZAP: up to about 4.32 million email addresses. J.League: about 2.38 million ticketing records (internal user IDs, birth dates, gender, prefecture and city). Stripe International: about 150,000 STRIPE CLUB records with email addresses and member IDs. Wacoal: internal IDs, ages, birth dates and gender possibly leaked (count still under investigation). NTT Solmare: up to about 1.17 million MangaPlaza records and up to about 130,000 Comic Cmoa records (hashed member numbers, birth dates, gender and more). Sharp: up to about 3.26 million online store customers' ages, birth dates and other details (NHK). PLAID also says credentials used to link KARTE to outside services leaked, and has asked customers to revoke them.

Status

Confirmed by PLAID and affected companies. KARTE still offline with no restart date as of Nov. 18; more companies announcing leaks as of Nov. 19.

About and methodology

How confirmed and speculated are decided

  • Confirmed Hacks were confirmed by the affected organization, a government agency, or law enforcement. Numbers come from those statements, filings, or the linked reporting. Attacker claims are labeled as claims.
  • Speculated / Potential Hacks are outages or incidents where the public wondered about a hack. They are not confirmed hacks. Each one shows what the organization or investigators officially said.

How this tracker is updated

  • The list is reviewed and updated about every two hours during the day, Eastern Time.
  • Sorted by scale and impact, biggest first. Severity is a rough editorial rating, not an official score.
  • Covers roughly the past 12 months, plus a few older events that are still widely referenced. Always check the linked sources for the latest.

The definition of hacking used on this site is at the top of the page, under What counts as hacking.
